Dioptres: a lens in air

A dioptre (D) measures how strongly a lens bends light. A stronger lens brings light to a point closer to it. This example is a single lens in air.

In air: power (D) = 1focal distance (m)

A +2 D lens focuses light 0.5 m away. A +4 D lens focuses it 0.25 m away. Minus lenses spread light out instead. The eye is about +60 D, but inside the eye light travels through fluid (n = 1.336), so the eye uses d = nV instead.

This is a simplified schematic model. The cornea and crystalline lens are combined into one thin lens at the front of the eye, and the light paths come from that single lens. Eye length is measured from that focusing plane to the retina, so it is not the same as a clinical axial-length measurement. The anatomy is drawn for orientation, and the space in front of the eye is compressed. The image counts as sharp when the focus is within about 0.15 D of the retina.

The simulation shows how a longer eye blurs distant vision, but not why an eye grows too long. Researchers have several overlapping ideas, and each has some support as well as important gaps.

Background: how the retina may steer eye growthShared by the ideas below, not a separate theory

Animal and human studies support the idea that what the eye sees helps regulate how it grows. Which visual cues matter most in people, and which chemical signals carry the message to the eye wall, remain unresolved, and no single pathway explains common myopia. IMI light review

Near work and accommodationLinked with myopia, but cause is unclear

IdeaLong periods of reading or screen use up close may encourage the eye to grow. Accommodation means the inner lens changing its power to focus on something close.

EvidenceNear work is often linked with myopia, but findings vary between studies. IMI light review A review of school-age children found that their maximum near-focusing ability was lower than an older age-based formula predicts, with statistically significant differences at ages 6, 9, 10 and 11. Accommodation review

Still uncertainThis review measured maximum near-focusing ability, not eye growth, so it cannot establish whether accommodation causes or predicts myopia. Accommodation review

DefocusAn eye-growth cue; human pathways remain under study

IdeaDefocus means light coming to a focus in front of or behind the retina. The eye may use the direction of blur as a signal to grow faster or slower.

EvidenceIn animals fitted with lenses that move the focus, the layer behind the retina quickly thins or thickens and the eye's growth later changes to match. In people, brief blur causes small, temporary changes in eye length. IMI light review

Still uncertainWhether these short-term human changes predict long-term growth is debated, and they do not establish the full cause of human myopia. IMI light review

Outdoor light and dopamineMost consistent for delaying onset

IdeaDaylight is far brighter than indoor light, and the light and views outdoors differ in other ways too. Bright light may slow eye growth by raising dopamine, a chemical messenger in the retina.

EvidenceTime outdoors is linked to fewer new cases of myopia in children. Outdoor exposure review Adding outdoor time at school slowed the onset of myopia in some trials, though not in all. IMI light review

Still uncertainEvidence for slowing myopia that has already started is less definitive. How bright light affects dopamine and eye growth is not settled, and dopamine's role has not been confirmed in people. Outdoor exposure review IMI light review

Contrast and ON/OFF pathwaysA proposed idea with early evidence

IdeaContrast is the pattern of light and dark in what we see. Cones in the retina pass signals to ON and OFF bipolar cells, which help detect contrast. The contrast theory, proposed by Neitz and colleagues, suggests that activity in these pathways helps regulate eye growth. Neitz contrast theory

EvidenceAnimal studies link ON and OFF pathway activity with eye growth. IMI light review

Still uncertainNo single contrast pathway has been established in people, so this remains a proposed explanation rather than an accepted one. IMI light review

Genetic susceptibilityAffects individual risk, together with environment

IdeaGenes affect how likely someone is to become near-sighted, partly by changing how they respond to their surroundings.

EvidenceMyopia runs in families, and over 1,000 common gene variants have been linked to refractive error. Some variants appear to act differently depending on education, a sign that genes and environment interact. IMI genetics report

Still uncertainThe known variants explain only part of the differences between people, and much of myopia's heritability is still unexplained. IMI genetics report
